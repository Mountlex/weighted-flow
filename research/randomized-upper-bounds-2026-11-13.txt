Randomized upper bounds for weighted flow time: research notes
13 November 2026

STATUS AND SCOPE

This is a research record, not a proposed manuscript addition. No improved
general upper bound was proved. The targets examined were poly(log log W)
and O(log log P), or more generally poly(log log P), for clairvoyant,
preemptive, unit-speed scheduling against an oblivious adversary.

The literature search located O(log min{W,P,D}) as the published baseline
in this model, from Azar--Touitou. It did not locate the requested improved
randomized guarantee. This is a search finding, not a proof of absence.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.10273

The SODA 2026 paper by Gupta--Kumar--Panigrahi--Wang gives a useful new
dual-fitting analysis of the weighted algorithm, but its main improved
guarantee concerns unweighted flow with uncertain job sizes.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611978971.47
Author PDF: https://users.cs.duke.edu/~debmalya/papers/soda26-flowtime.pdf

1. A PRECISE DUAL-FITTING TARGET

Let K be the number of rounded weight classes, so K=O(1+log W).
The weighted analysis in Section 6 of the SODA 2026 paper constructs a
dual objective covering the maximum unfinished weight of one class.
Its congestion at any job j is at most 11 w_j. The K factor enters when
bounding the sum of all class backlogs by K times the largest backlog.
Improving the constant 11 would therefore not improve the asymptotics.

Here is a sufficient expected-dual statement. Fix the entire input I
and a time t. All jobs below are released by t, and sets S are nonempty.
For random seed omega, allow a reduced instance with
p_j^omega <= p_j and the same releases and weights. For a set S put

  e_omega(S) = max{0, sum_{j in S} p_j^omega - (t-min_{j in S} r_j)}.

For nonnegative random dual variables y_S^omega, define

  D_omega = sum_S e_omega(S) y_S^omega,
  L_j^omega = sum_{S containing j}
                min{p_j^omega,e_omega(S)} y_S^omega.

If

  E D_omega >= E U_A(I,t),
  E L_j^omega <= c w_j  for every fixed original job j,

then E U_A(I,t) <= c U_O(I,t) for every fixed comparison schedule O.
Indeed, O's survivor indicators z_j are feasible for every reduced
instance's knapsack-cover inequalities. Therefore, pathwise,

  D_omega <= sum_j z_j L_j^omega.

Taking expectations gives the result because z_j is independent of the
algorithm's seed. Integrating over t yields a competitive bound.

Thus pathwise dual feasibility is unnecessary. A target for an improved
algorithm is to cover the SUM of expected class backlogs while giving
each fixed job only poly(log K) expected dual load.

The missing part is substantial: no online policy and dual construction
with those properties was found. One cannot keep the existing algorithm
and assume its K loss vanishes under randomization. Also, after averaging
over a distribution of INPUTS, z_j depends on the input; an unconditional
bound on average dual load cannot simply be multiplied by E z_j.

For the size target, K=O(1+log P) classes would make an O(log K) loss into
O(1+log log P). A balanced hierarchy has this depth, but that geometric
observation alone supplies no competitive analysis.

2. STATIC BATCHES ALREADY ADMIT CONSTANT FACTORS

Suppose all jobs are available at time zero. Write p(S), w(S) for total
size and weight. In reverse time, a target volume q has optimum cost

  C(q) = min{w(S): p(S) >= q}.

For budgets B_i = w_min 2^i, choose S_i maximizing p(S_i) subject to
w(S_i)<=B_i. Append all previously unlisted jobs of S_i, then continue
to the next budget. For the first i with B_i>=C(q), at least q volume
has been listed, while the total listed weight is less than

  sum_{h<=i} B_h < 2 B_i <= 4 C(q).

Reverse this order. Its unfinished weight at EVERY deadline is at most
four times the deadline optimum. The argument uses exact knapsack
oracles. Rounding weights down in units epsilon B/n gives a polynomial
oracle with volume at least the exact B-budget optimum and weight at
most (1+epsilon)B, giving factor 4(1+epsilon).

Budgets B_i=w_min exp(i+U), with U uniform on [0,1], instead give expected
factor e. For each fixed C, the expected sum of geometric bids through
the first bid at least C is at most e C. With weight rounding one obtains
e(1+epsilon). This is the standard randomized online-bidding calculation,
not a novelty claim.
Source: Chrobak--Kenyon--Noga--Young, Theorem 4:
https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~neal/publication/Chrobak06Online.pdf

A constant-factor static deadline schedule was already known: Becchetti
et al., JDA 2006, Theorem 12 gives factor 24. Their conclusion explicitly
suggests recomputing a residual schedule at each release. Thus simply
proposing recomputation would not be a new algorithmic insight.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jda.2005.12.001

The true difficulty is making such decisions consistent across arrivals.
There is no competitive proof here for recomputing these orders online.

3. WHY LOCAL RANDOMIZED OPTIMA DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY COUPLE

All jobs are released at zero. Let a,b have (p,w)=(2,1), and c have
(p,w)=(3,2). At time 2 prescribe the completed-set distribution that
chooses {a} or {b} with equal probability. At time 4 prescribe the
distribution that chooses {a,b} or {c} with equal probability.

Both distributions are supported on feasible, locally optimal completed
sets. Every job's completion probability is nondecreasing. Nevertheless
no random schedule realizes both distributions: on every sample path,
a or b has completed by time 2, which prevents c from completing by
time 4 because their combined processing requirement is 5.

Consequently, locally feasible completion marginals plus monotonicity
are insufficient. A rounding argument must respect the distribution
of residual processing states and how it evolves through time.

An equivalent Bayesian pitfall is to resample a clairvoyant optimal
continuation when an arrival reveals information. The new posterior
may require changing work that has already been done. An explicit
coupling or repair-cost argument is needed.

4. A SIMPLE COUNTEREXAMPLE TO FRESH RANDOM HIERARCHIES

Consider a binary node whose left child has smaller jobs. Let L,R be
the total unfinished weights in its children. At each completion,
choose left with probability min{1,L/R}, otherwise right, and run the
chosen job to completion. Use SRPT within each leaf class. This is
equivalent to a fresh Pareto bias
on the smaller-size child.

At time zero release one left job (p,w)=(1,1) and n right jobs
(p,w)=(2,M), with M>=2. While q right jobs remain and left has not
been selected, the next selection chooses left with probability
1/(qM). Hence

  Pr[left is served before all right jobs]
    = 1 - product_{q=1}^n (1-1/(qM))
    >= 1 - exp(-H_n/M).

At the FIXED deadline T=2n, OPT completes every right job and leaves
weight 1. If the algorithm served left, it leaves a right job of
weight M with one unit of remaining processing. Taking n=ceil(exp M)
therefore gives expected robust backlog Omega(M).

The deadline instance has P=2. To turn this into a flow-time lower
bound, add a sufficiently long flooding stream of jobs of weight 1/2
and size 1/(2M), released back-to-back starting at T. The robust backlog
argument then gives expected competitive loss Omega(M). In the FINAL
flow instance P=4M, W=2M, and D=M. In particular, this defeats the
proposed algorithm's poly(log log P) and poly(log log W) guarantees.
It does NOT establish a flow lower bound at P=2.

Resampling at half-processing milestones also does not repair this
example: before the first left selection, all full-right-completion
opportunities remain; a selected left job receives at least half a
unit of work. The remaining heavy work at T is then at least half a
unit, concentrated in one unfinished right job by within-class SRPT,
and the flooding scale changes only by a constant factor.

This counterexample identifies why unequal completion weights matter.
Each right completion removes weight M but gives the light job another
independent chance. The accumulated probability is harmonic in n.

5. OTHER NEGATIVE CHECKS

These are obstructions to specific families, not impossibility results
for the desired bounds.

* Fixed, input-independent random multipliers on weight-class backlogs,
  with fixed tie-breaking, do not suffice. With K classes of equal total
  weight, choose uniformly
  which class contains long jobs; all other classes contain short jobs.
  The long class blocks all classes below it in the fixed random rank.
  Averaging its rank gives Omega(K) unfinished weight relative to OPT.

* Fixed, once-sampled iid positive per-job multiplicative perturbations
  of remaining density fail already with two jobs. A short (p,w)=(1,2)
  job and a long (W,W) job have a constant chance of being processed
  short-first. At T=W this leaves weight W instead of 2.

* Proportional sharing among weight classes can fail even for bounded
  weight ratio: n unit jobs of weight 1 and a long job of weight 2
  leave Theta(log n) small-job backlog at T=n under fluid sharing.
  Infinitesimally frequent resampling with those same service
  probabilities has the same service-loss issue.

* Fixed local biases in a binary size hierarchy incur products of
  losses along a path. Independent fixed Pareto biases also admit
  a multilevel obstruction with exponentially growing expected backlog
  in the depth. Refreshing only when total node mass halves retains
  that obstruction: on the bad event the relevant masses never halve.

Small numerical tests initially made some hierarchical rules look
promising. The explicit multiplicity example above overturns those
small-instance observations; they should not be cited as support for
a competitive conjecture.

6. WHAT CAN BE PROVED IN A RESTRICTED MERGER

For integer 0<=L<=R, consider transitions

  (L,R) -> (L-1,R) with probability L/R,
  (L,R) -> (L,R-1) otherwise.

This is an urn with R balls, L blue: selecting a blue ball turns it
red; selecting a red ball deletes it. Give every ball independent
rate-one exponential clocks. From initial state (L_0,R_0),

  E L(t)=L_0 exp(-t),
  E R(t)=(R_0+L_0 t) exp(-t).

For 1<=r<=R_0/2, let tau_r be the first time R=r. Choosing t so that
E R(t)=2r and applying a lower-tail Chernoff bound gives

  E L(tau_r)
    <= 2r/[1+ln(R_0/(2r))] + L_0 exp(-r/4).

Proof: Pr(tau_r<t)<=exp(-r/4), L decreases, and
L_0 exp(-t)=2r/(R_0/L_0+t), with t>=ln(R_0/(2r)). If L_0=0 the
conclusion is immediate. This is a rigorous logarithmic suppression
effect, but it only models EQUAL weight decrements. Section 4 explains
why it does not extend automatically to weighted job completions.

Another easy special case: highest-weight-first is 2b-competitive for
power-of-two weights if at most b jobs of each weight are pending in
its schedule at any time. For every weight threshold it minimizes
the unfinished PROCESSING VOLUME above the threshold. Hence its
maximum pending weight is at most OPT's maximum pending weight.
Summing at most b jobs per geometric class gives the 2b bound.
This leaves the large-multiplicity case unresolved.

7. STABILITY OF KNAPSACK PROFILES IS A CONCRETE OPEN STEP

The union of optimal knapsack sets is not stable under arrivals.
Initially let a=(p,w)=(X,1), b=(X+1/2,2), with X>1.
At budgets 1 and 2, the maximizing sets are {a} and {b}, whose union
has volume 2X+1/2. Add c=(1,1). The maximizing sets become {a} and
{a,c}; their union has volume X+1. A size-one arrival has reduced the
union-volume profile by X-1/2, arbitrarily large.

A restricted repair is to retain a budget-B set until a replacement
improves volume by a factor 1+epsilon. If A is arrival volume and Q
is service given to the retained set since its last exact update,
then its discarded current volume at such a replacement is at most
(A+Q)/epsilon. For frozen processing sizes, Q=0.

However, summing this charge over geometric budgets can charge the
same arrival or processing many times, recovering a logarithmic
parameter loss. No bound reducing that accumulation to log log W
or log log P was proved.

A single merge is easier: at the time of merging residual batches,
recompute a c-universal static order on their union. After any amount
of subsequent cumulative service, its residual weight is at most c
times that under any allocation of the same service among the old
batches. But applying this comparison repeatedly can multiply c once
per merge. A useful merging theorem must instead make losses additive
across levels or telescope through a potential.

8. MOST CONCRETE NEXT PROOF TARGET

Try persistent random processing thresholds with priorities independent
of the random execution, and analyze their conditional tails via a
one-random-coordinate monotonicity lemma. The original RMLF analysis
offers a proof template: after all other randomness is fixed, survival
is a downward-closed event in a job's promotion threshold. This handles
the correlation between random choices and jobs remaining alive.
Source: Becchetti--Leonardi, JACM 2004, Lemmas 7--8:
https://doi.org/10.1145/1008731.1008732

In abstract form, if E={T<=b}, b<=p, and the CDF satisfies
F(p-delta)>=cF(p), then Pr(T<=p-delta | E)>=c. The missing work is
to enforce such a monotonicity property under weighted arrivals and
use it to prove the expected dual-congestion bound in Section 1.

This is a research direction, not an algorithm with an established
ratio. The nonclairvoyant RMLF theorem itself has an O(log n) bound
for unweighted jobs and does not give the desired weighted result.
Its tail parameters depend on the arrival index to control rare events;
replacing n by the number of classes is unjustified when one class may
contain arbitrarily many jobs.

9. COMPUTATIONAL CHECKS AND LIMITS

Scratch programs in /tmp used exact integer-time offline dynamic
programming on small instances, exhaustive subset knapsack oracles,
and a finite arrival-prefix minimax game. The static oracle was checked
against every integer deadline on random seven-job batches. The small
prefix game reached primal/dual agreement at 11/8 for one depth-three
restricted family; larger runs did not close their gap. These are
checks of restricted computations, not general upper bounds.

Scratch source names: wf_upper_search.cpp, wf_tree_search.cpp,
wf_static_oracle.py, wf_deadline_game.py. The mathematical conclusions
above do not depend on extrapolating numerical growth rates.

CONCLUSION

Neither target was resolved. The useful outputs are a precise expected
dual certificate, a constant-factor static primitive, explicit failures
of several random-priority families, and a clearer identification of the
unresolved issue: maintaining a causally valid schedule and controlling
random decisions when jobs have very different weights and arrive later.
